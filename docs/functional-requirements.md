# Declan functional specification and roadmap

**Draft 0.2 — for owner review, NOT approved.** Source: `main` after PR #45 merged, 8 November 2026. This document defines the candidate requirements baseline and planned direction. No code change or live HA deployment is authorised by approving a document PR alone.

**Status key:** **Source** = implemented in repository, subject to test/live verification; **Planned** = proposed future behaviour; **Decision** = requires explicit owner agreement. An implemented module does not prove it is deployed, configured or working in the household.

## 1. Product, identity and configuration

- **FR-001 [Source]** Home Voice Assistant shall register with Home Assistant under the `declan_local` integration domain and expose a conversation agent, settings and administrator panel.
- **FR-002 [Source]** The assistant shall be called Declan. Its personality and display branding shall be distinct from stable HA entity IDs and storage identifiers.
- **FR-003 [Source]** Device control shall use existing HA entities, exposed areas, services and scripts; it shall not silently create or replace household YAML or automations.
- **FR-004 [Source]** Optional mappings for switches, satellites, music players, car sensors, thermostat, weather and guest helpers shall validate entity type and configured availability.
- **FR-005 [Decision]** All remaining Jarvis references shall be reviewed using [the audit](legacy-jarvis-reference-audit.md), not assumed necessary for compatibility. The owner reports that all live HA Jarvis references have already been renamed.
- **FR-006 [Planned]** Future migrations shall preserve settings, voice caches, speaker profiles, learning history and guest-authority ownership, with upgrade/rollback tests.

### Portability and native Home Assistant configuration (new candidates; Phase 1 audit)

- **FR-007 [Planned]** A clean HA installation shall be able to configure Declan without editing integration source code or inheriting another household's person, camera, device, network or script identifiers. Missing optional features shall not block core setup.
- **FR-008 [Planned]** Declan shall discover available HA entities/areas and offer native HA selectors for mappings. The native options flow and Declan dashboard shall share one authoritative persisted configuration, with validation and clear missing-entity handling.
- **FR-009 [Planned]** Declan shall route configurable routines to user-selected native HA scripts or automations; optional examples/blueprints shall not be installed or enabled silently. Execution results shall follow FR-063 when that contract is approved.
- **FR-086 [Planned]** Declan shall support versioned configuration export/import with schema validation, dry-run preview, entity remapping, explicit conflict approval and rollback. Default exports shall exclude credentials, tokens, speaker biometrics, private learning evidence and personal playback history.
- **FR-087 [Planned]** Persisted storage and registry migrations shall be idempotent and non-destructive, retaining legacy reads until verified. A clean install and an upgrade from an existing household shall both pass acceptance tests.
- **FR-088 [Planned]** Optional integrations (cameras, Frigate, Music Assistant/Spotify, EV, guest helpers) shall be individually configurable and safely unavailable when absent.

**Evidence:** [Phase 1 portability audit](portability-hardcoded-values-audit.md) is an initial targeted scan on `main`; full repository coverage and acceptance tests remain open. Settings dashboard work is separate draft PR #49; portability audit is draft PR #50. These requirements are proposed, not approved or implemented.

## 2. Personality and privacy

- **FR-010 [Source]** **Only Guest Mode selects the persona**: `off` = savage Home; `on` = playful Guest; missing/unknown/unavailable = privacy-safe Guest.
- **FR-011 [Source]** Visitors, speaker identity, owner presence and quiet hours shall not directly choose a persona. **Owner-approved change:** a confirmed overnight-staying declaration shall enable the configured Guest Mode helper, which then selects the Guest persona; the declaration itself does not become an independent persona selector. Automatic handover may also change the helper under its own eligibility rules.
- **FR-012 [Source]** Home speech may include strong personal banter for Jaco and Edd; Guest speech may roast visitors lightly but shall avoid private host details, intimate jokes and personalised host greetings.
- **FR-013 [Source]** Serious safety, security, medical, operational failure and factual messages shall be clear, accurate and not replaced by jokes.
- **FR-014 [Source]** Quiet hours shall not turn off sass or override Guest Mode. Any volume/delivery policy shall be defined separately.
- **FR-015 [Source]** Confirmations, social greetings, corrections, clarification and cached category alternatives shall respect the effective Guest/Home persona, without changing the factual outcome or exposing private Home audio in Guest Mode.
- **FR-016 [Source]** Speaker identity may personalise suitable Home greetings, but shall never confer permissions or independently select persona.
- **FR-020A [Owner-approved personality policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall **not automatically reduce or disable sass** based on inferred frustration, serious discussion or troubleshooting context. The active Home/Guest personality remains governed by Guest Mode (FR-010–011); conversational tone detection must not silently switch personas or suppress the established personality. Factual accuracy, privacy, safety-critical clarity and operational failure reporting remain mandatory (FR-012–015), and any explicit user instruction to be brief or stop joking may be honoured for that interaction without changing the underlying persona. Test that frustration and troubleshooting do not cause automatic personality changes.
- **FR-017 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** During configurable quiet hours, Declan shall lower spoken output volume and shorten non-critical responses while retaining the persona selected solely by Guest Mode. Quiet-hours settings shall default to **23:00–05:00 local Home Assistant time** (crossing midnight), while allowing the user to edit the schedule, reduced volume and concise-response policy. **Owner-approved default quiet reply volume: 25%**, configurable per installation. Before applying a temporary quiet reply volume, capture the originating output player's current volume and restore that previous value after the response finishes, including on error or cancellation where technically possible; do not overwrite a volume change made by the user in the meantime. Define safe behaviour for players that cannot report/set volume, overlapping playback and announcements during implementation. Safety-critical information must remain clear and audible; exact defaults and volume restoration behaviour require implementation design and tests.
- **FR-019 [Owner-approved behaviour; feasibility pending]** When the originating voice input is reliably detected as whispered, Declan shall treat **that interaction** as quiet regardless of the clock: reply locally at reduced volume and with a shorter non-critical response, without changing the configured quiet-hours schedule, global announcement routing, or Guest/Home persona. Whisper detection must use an explicit, tested signal or classifier with a confidence threshold; do not infer whispering merely from low microphone amplitude, which varies with distance, microphone gain and noise reduction. If whisper detection is unavailable or uncertain, fall back to the ordinary schedule and local reply policy. Do not persist quiet mode beyond the interaction solely because of a whisper. Safety-critical responses remain intelligible. Detection approach, thresholds and device support are implementation decisions.
- **FR-018 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Quiet-hours proactive announcements and broadcast responses shall be restricted to a user-configurable allowlist of one or more Home Assistant announcement-capable media players/Shows. For the owner’s household, the intended initial selection is the **master-bedroom Echo Show only**; this is a household setting, never a hardcoded default for other installations. Settings shall offer native HA device/entity selection, validate availability, and avoid silently rerouting to unselected devices. The policy applies only to proactive/unsolicited announcements and broadcasts. **Owner-approved direct-request exception:** a voice request made at any Assist satellite during quiet hours shall receive its spoken response locally at that originating satellite, at reduced quiet-hours volume, rather than being redirected to the announcement allowlist. Direct requests shall remain usable in other rooms; the announcement allowlist must not suppress or reroute their local replies. Empty or unavailable allowlists must fail safely without broadcasting house-wide.

## 3. Local command interpretation and actions

- **FR-020 [Source]** Commands shall be parsed and validated before actions; unknown or ambiguous devices shall not be guessed.
- **FR-021 [Source]** Lighting, brightness, areas and configured switch-as-light aliases shall support room-aware commands and bounded follow-ups.
- **FR-022 [Source]** Good Morning, Good Night and Movie Time shall invoke the corresponding configured HA routines when available.
- **FR-023 [Source]** Movie Time responses shall distinguish confirmed action from unknown/unconfirmed outcome; no false claim of projector success.
- **FR-024 [Source]** In-sentence corrections shall execute only the final validated request, not superseded commands.
- **FR-025 [Source]** Follow-up references shall be conversation-scoped, time-bounded and revalidated.
- **FR-031 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall retain active conversational context for **five minutes after the most recent interaction** in that conversation, resetting the inactivity timeout on each subsequent interaction. Within that window, context may resolve relevant follow-up references (for example, “turn on the lounge lights” followed by “actually, dim them to 30%”), provided the referent remains unambiguous and the target and action are revalidated before execution. After five minutes without an interaction, the active context shall expire; Declan must not silently apply expired context to a new request. Context retention shall not grant permissions, override Guest Mode privacy restrictions, or bypass command validation. Context scope across rooms/speakers, persistence across restart, and long-term learning remain separate decisions. Add acceptance tests for follow-ups within the window, inactivity expiry, timeout reset, ambiguous references and security boundaries.
- **FR-032 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Conversational context shall follow a **reliably recognised speaker across configured room satellites** within the five-minute rolling inactivity window (FR-031), so a follow-up in another room may refer to the same ongoing conversation. Context shall be **isolated per recognised speaker**: Jaco's and Edd's conversations must not be mixed, and one speaker must not inherit another's prior targets, private details or pending commands. Speaker identity must not grant permissions or override Guest Mode restrictions (FR-016, FR-046). If identity is unknown, ambiguous or low-confidence, Declan shall not attach the request to a named speaker's existing context; use a suitably isolated session or request clarification instead. Revalidate device targets and room-dependent references when the satellite changes, rather than assuming a previous room applies. Cross-room handoff, identity confidence, simultaneous speech, shared/guest sessions and restart behaviour require implementation design and tests.
- **FR-033 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** When a speaker cannot be reliably identified, Declan shall allow **temporary conversational context scoped to the originating room/satellite session**, with the same five-minute rolling inactivity expiry as FR-031. This permits ordinary local follow-ups (for example, “turn on the lights” then “make them brighter”) without requiring speaker recognition. Unknown or low-confidence speakers shall **never inherit, read, modify or merge into** Jaco's, Edd's or any other identified speaker's conversation context; their temporary context shall not roam across rooms or satellites. Separate simultaneous unidentified sessions when technically possible; if safe separation is not possible, ask for clarification rather than using potentially mixed context. Guest Mode privacy, restricted actions and device/permission validation apply equally. Expired temporary context shall be discarded, and later speaker identification must not retroactively attach an unknown session to a named user's private history. Add acceptance tests for follow-ups, room isolation, expiry, mixed-speaker ambiguity and privacy boundaries.
- **FR-034 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall recognise natural in-utterance corrections, such as “Turn on the kitchen lights. Actually, no, the dining room lights”, and execute **only the final corrected, validated intent**. Superseded intents must not cause any side effects; if the final intent is ambiguous, clarify before acting. This refines FR-024 and requires regression tests covering correction phrasing, negation, multiple corrections and action safety.
- **FR-035 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** When Declan is speaking, an explicit interruption such as “Declan, stop” shall **promptly stop the current spoken response** on the relevant output device without undoing household actions already completed. Stopping speech must not silently cancel or roll back completed actions; pending or in-progress actions require explicit, separately specified cancellation semantics and must not be falsely reported as completed. Distinguish stop-speaking intent from the existing media-playback stop command (FR-028), using active playback context and clarification when ambiguous. Preserve privacy, room targeting and quiet-hours delivery rules. Test interruption latency, output routing, overlapping media and action-state reporting.
- **FR-036 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **controlled long-term learning of useful household and per-speaker preferences** across conversations and days, distinct from the five-minute active context window (FR-031). For example, an explicit statement such as “I prefer the lounge lights at 30% in the evening” may be stored as a preference and used in later relevant interactions, subject to speaker identity, context, current device state and authorisation. Users shall be able to **inspect, correct and delete** learned preferences and opt out of retention; Declan shall not silently treat guesses, transient commands or low-confidence inferences as permanent preferences. Store provenance and scope (person versus household), apply Guest Mode privacy restrictions and prevent guests or unidentified speakers from accessing or overwriting private owner preferences. Never allow learned preferences to override explicit current commands, safety limits, access restrictions or device validation. Retention policy, consent/confirmation UX, storage schema, and cross-device synchronisation remain implementation decisions; add tests for persistence, correction/deletion, privacy, conflicting preferences and safe fallback.
- **FR-037 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **both explicit and suggested preference learning**. (1) **Explicit learning:** when an authorised speaker clearly asks Declan to remember a preference (for example, “Declan, remember that I prefer the lounge lights at 30% in the evening”), Declan shall validate the request, determine whether it is personal or household-scoped, persist the preference where permitted, and acknowledge only confirmed storage. Ambiguous scope or sensitive content requires clarification or explicit consent; guests must not write owner-private preferences. (2) **Suggested learning:** Declan may identify repeated, sufficiently reliable patterns from permitted observations and offer a concise, personality-appropriate suggestion (for example, “You keep setting the lounge lights to 30% in the evening. Want me to remember that?”). **No suggested preference shall be saved or applied as a standing rule without explicit user approval.** Suggestions shall be infrequent, dismissible, reviewable, and avoid interruptions during quiet hours or sensitive interactions; a rejection must not be treated as consent. Both methods shall support inspection, correction, deletion and opt-out under FR-036, and must not bypass security, Guest Mode privacy, speaker separation or device validation. Pattern thresholds, suggestion frequency and data-retention details require implementation design and tests.
- **FR-038 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **learning corrected command interpretations** when an authorised speaker explicitly corrects a misunderstood request (for example, “No, Declan, when I say country music, I mean my Country playlist”). Declan shall identify the proposed phrase-to-intent or phrase-to-target mapping, explain it back in plain language and **request explicit confirmation before persisting** any new interpretation. An unconfirmed, ambiguous or rejected correction shall not create a permanent mapping. Confirmed mappings shall be scoped appropriately to a speaker or household, retain provenance, be reviewable/editable/removable and survive restart under FR-036–037. Mappings must not override higher-priority explicit commands, bypass permissions, Guest Mode privacy or security restrictions, or silently redirect commands to unsafe or unavailable targets. If the correction concerns the current request, execution shall occur only after normal intent/target validation and must not assume the new mapping is already saved. Add tests for confirmation, rejection, conflicting mappings, speaker isolation, safe execution and rollback.
- **FR-039 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan may offer **proactive, contextually useful assistance** based on reliable current household data and approved preferences (for example, a reminder that the Peugeot is not plugged in ahead of its usual charging window, or a suggestion to adjust heating when the house is unusually cold). Proactive suggestions shall be **opt-in/configurable**, with user-controlled categories, frequency/rate limits, and the ability to dismiss, snooze or disable a suggestion type. Declan must verify relevant sensor states, timing, presence and data freshness before speaking; uncertain or unavailable data must not be presented as fact. Respect Guest Mode privacy and permissions, the 23:00–05:00 quiet-hours policy and configured proactive announcement destinations (FR-017–019), and avoid unnecessary interruptions. **Suggestions and reminders shall never execute household actions without explicit authorisation** and normal action validation. Avoid duplicate alerts and record safe, reviewable diagnostics without exposing sensitive details. Trigger thresholds, consent UX and delivery/deduplication tests remain implementation work.
- **FR-040 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan's **five-minute active conversational context is ephemeral** and **may reset on a Home Assistant restart**. There is no requirement to persist or restore in-progress dialogue sessions, unresolved references or temporary guest/satellite context across restarts. After restart, Declan shall start a fresh session and ask for clarification rather than assuming a prior conversational referent. This does **not** delete separately confirmed long-term preferences or learned command interpretations under FR-036–038, which remain persistent according to their own retention rules. Test restart isolation and preservation of approved long-term memory.
- **FR-041 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall understand **multiple independent commands in a single utterance** (for example, “turn off the kitchen lights, set the lounge lights to 30%, and play country music”). Parse each requested action and its target separately, respecting natural conjunctions, incidental punctuation and corrections (FR-030, FR-034). Validate permissions, target resolution and parameters **per action** before execution; ambiguous or unsafe actions require clarification and must not be guessed. Execute independent valid actions without allowing a failure in one device/service to prevent the others, while respecting explicit ordering or dependencies when the user specifies them. Report partial success accurately, identifying which actions succeeded or failed, without claiming the whole request completed if it did not. Never automatically retry non-idempotent actions or roll back successful unrelated actions without authorisation. Add tests for mixed domains (lights/music/heating), partial failures, ordering, corrections, permission boundaries and clear confirmation.
- **FR-042 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **delayed and scheduled one-off voice commands**, including relative times (for example, “turn off the lounge lights in 20 minutes”) and explicit clock times (for example, “at 10 tonight, turn off the TV and downstairs lights”). Interpret times in the household's configured local timezone, resolve ambiguous dates/times by clarification rather than guessing, and confirm the intended action(s) and execution time before accepting a schedule. Pending commands shall be **listable and cancellable by voice**, including a clear distinction between cancelling one command and all commands. Persist accepted pending jobs across Home Assistant restarts where safe and supported, independently of the ephemeral five-minute conversational context (FR-040). At execution time, revalidate targets, permissions, device availability and safety constraints; do not execute commands whose authorisation has been revoked, and report execution failures without claiming success. Respect Guest Mode privacy and permissions, including preventing guest access to private owners' schedules. Prevent duplicate execution, apply appropriate expiry/missed-run rules, and avoid announcing completion during quiet hours unless allowed by notification settings. Tests shall cover daylight-saving transitions, restart recovery, cancellation, multiple actions, missed execution, duplicate prevention and security.
- **FR-043 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **conditional voice requests** that monitor a reliable Home Assistant state or event and respond when the condition is met, including **notification-only rules** (for example, “When the washing machine finishes, let me know”) and **conditional action rules** (for example, “If the temperature drops below 18°C, turn the heating on”). Distinguish the two: a notification-only rule shall not perform a device action; a conditional action rule requires **explicit authorisation at creation**, including clear confirmation of the trigger, action, target and duration or expiry. Validate that the trigger can actually be observed with available entities and trustworthy data; do not pretend an unsupported sensor or appliance-finished signal exists. Ask for clarification on ambiguous thresholds, locations, recipients or duration. Pending rules shall be reviewable, listable, cancellable and disableable by voice, with appropriately authorised ownership and Guest Mode privacy. Revalidate permissions, sensor freshness, target availability and safety constraints at trigger time; prevent repeated triggering or loops using defined edge/threshold and cooldown behaviour. Handle Home Assistant restart, stale or unavailable sensors, missed events, notification quiet hours and failed actions without false success claims. Tests shall cover both rule types, approval, cancellation, duplicate triggers, stale sensors, restart recovery, privacy and failure reporting. Exact rule expiry and trigger policy remain implementation-design decisions.
- **FR-044 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **recurring voice-created schedules and reminders** using natural-language calendar patterns (for example, “every weekday at 7 AM, turn on the kitchen lights” and “every Sunday evening, remind me to put the bins out”). Before activation, confirm the recurrence pattern, local time, target or recipient, action/reminder content and any ambiguous details. Provide voice commands to **list, inspect, edit, pause/resume and delete** individual recurring rules, with explicit confirmation for destructive or security-sensitive changes. Persist authorised recurring rules across Home Assistant restarts, independently of ephemeral conversational context (FR-040), and handle local timezone and daylight-saving transitions deterministically. Revalidate authorisation, Guest Mode privacy, target availability and safety constraints on each run; distinguish notification-only reminders from device actions, and never claim an action succeeded without confirmation. Avoid duplicate execution, overlapping runs and runaway recurrence; define missed-run handling, expiry and sensible quiet-hours delivery consistent with FR-017–019. Guest-created rules must not silently acquire owner permissions or expose private schedules. Add tests for recurrence parsing, updates, cancellation, restart recovery, DST, duplicate prevention, permissions and partial failures. Implementation and deployment remain out of scope for this draft.
- **FR-045 [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall understand **follow-up instructions that modify an action just performed**, resolving references such as “them”, “it”, “that room” and “a bit dimmer” from the active, speaker-isolated five-minute conversation context (FR-031–033). For example, after “turn on the lounge lights”, “actually, make them a bit dimmer” shall target those lounge lights without requiring the room to be restated, provided the referent is unambiguous and the target remains valid. Relative adjustments shall use current verified device state and supported capabilities, apply bounded changes and avoid guessing unsupported values. When several recent targets could match, context has expired, the speaker changes without verified identity, or the device is unavailable, Declan shall ask for clarification rather than acting on a guess. A follow-up instruction is a new action, not an automatic rollback of prior successful actions; validate authorisation, Guest Mode permissions, safety and device availability before execution. Honour explicit corrections under FR-034 and report failures without false success claims. Tests shall cover pronoun resolution, relative changes, multi-target ambiguity, cross-room continuity, speaker isolation, expired context and device state changes.
- **FR-045A [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall understand **contextual follow-up information questions** within the existing five-minute, speaker-isolated conversation window (FR-031–033), carrying forward the subject, requested attribute and comparison context when unambiguous. For example, after “What's the temperature in the lounge?”, “And the bedroom?” shall be interpreted as a request for the bedroom temperature, without requiring the user to repeat “temperature”. Each answer must retrieve **fresh, appropriate Home Assistant sensor data** for the newly referenced location and distinguish unavailable or stale data from a verified reading; never reuse a previous room's reading as if it belonged to the new room. Support natural follow-ups such as “What about upstairs?” or “Is that warmer than the lounge?” where the target and measurement are clear. Clarify ambiguous room, device or attribute references rather than guessing. Respect speaker separation, Guest Mode privacy and permissions, expired context and restart reset (FR-040); do not infer private owner context for an unidentified speaker. Maintain the active personality without sacrificing factual accuracy. Test subject carryover, room changes, comparisons, stale sensors, ambiguous references, speaker changes and context expiry.
- **FR-045B [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** For **multi-action utterances containing an in-sentence change of mind**, Declan shall resolve the complete utterance and apply corrections **before executing any affected action**, preserving only the final intended set of commands. Example: “Turn off the kitchen lights and the lounge lights. Actually, leave the lounge lights on” shall turn off the kitchen lights but **shall not turn off the lounge lights at all**. A correction that cancels or replaces one action must not discard unrelated valid actions; repeated or conflicting corrections shall be resolved in spoken order, subject to clarification if final intent remains ambiguous. This extends FR-034 (in-utterance corrections) and FR-041 (multiple commands); it must not rely on executing earlier actions then undoing them. Validate all retained actions normally, report partial failures accurately, and test mixed commands, negations, final-intent resolution and no side effects from superseded actions.
- **FR-045C [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall understand **natural-language exceptions and exclusions in grouped commands**, such as “Turn off all the downstairs lights except the dining room” and “Turn off everything upstairs, but leave the bedroom fan running”. Resolve the requested area, device classes and **explicit exclusions before executing any actions**; excluded entities must never be included in the action target set or temporarily toggled. Respect the difference between a room exclusion and an individual-device exclusion, and between “lights” and broader “everything” requests. Determine current entities and their capabilities from Home Assistant rather than relying on stale hardcoded device lists. If a target, room membership, device category or exception is ambiguous, ask for clarification before potentially affecting it. Revalidate Guest Mode permissions, safety and device availability for each included entity; do not allow broad commands to bypass protected-device restrictions. Support combined inclusion/exclusion and last-minute corrections (FR-034, FR-041, FR-045B), with accurate partial-failure reporting. Tests shall verify excluded devices have **zero side effects**, including mixed light/fan requests, dynamic entity membership, multiple exclusions, ambiguity and unavailable devices.
- **FR-045D [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall resolve **relative room references** in voice commands using the originating voice satellite's configured Home Assistant area: “in here”, “this room” and equivalent phrases shall refer to that area when the satellite-to-area mapping is verified. For phrases such as “the room next door”, “nearby” or “the adjacent room”, use an explicitly configured and reliable room-adjacency model only where a single target is unambiguous; if multiple rooms qualify or no adjacency data exists, **ask for clarification before executing**. Never infer adjacency solely from device names, stale assumptions or the last room mentioned. Revalidate the resolved target, device capabilities, current Home Assistant area membership, permissions and Guest Mode restrictions before action. If the originating satellite has no reliable assigned area, clarify rather than guessing. Support multiple commands, exceptions and corrections (FR-041, FR-045B–C) without changing explicitly named room targets. Tests shall cover satellite area mappings, satellite relocation, missing or ambiguous adjacency, cross-room conversation continuity, named-room overrides, and safe clarification.
- **FR-045E [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall understand **natural-language room groups and floor-level descriptions** in commands, including “all the bedrooms”, “upstairs”, “downstairs” and user-defined household room groups. Resolve group membership from **current Home Assistant area/floor assignments and explicitly configured custom group definitions**, rather than hardcoded entity lists; custom groups may overlap and shall be reviewable/configurable by authorised household users. For example, “turn off the lights in all the bedrooms” shall select only light entities in areas belonging to the bedrooms group; “turn off everything downstairs except the kitchen” shall first resolve the downstairs group, then exclude the kitchen area and all its devices **before any action**. Respect device class, area and floor distinctions, group exceptions (FR-045C), relative locations (FR-045D), multi-action corrections (FR-045B), and Guest Mode permissions. Clarify missing, ambiguous or conflicting group membership rather than guessing, and never include protected or unauthorised entities merely because the user said “everything”. Revalidate current targets and capabilities before execution and report partial failures accurately. Tests shall cover dynamic membership changes, overlapping custom groups, floor mapping, excluded rooms, broad-action safety, and zero side effects on excluded devices.
- **FR-045F [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **multiple natural-language nicknames and aliases per Home Assistant device**, independent of the entity's technical identifier. For example, “turn on the skylight” may resolve to the household's Artika Skyshade light when an authorised user has established that alias. Declan shall support **voice-based alias learning**, but must confirm the intended device, alias and scope (personal or household) **before saving**; do not silently learn aliases from uncertain speech recognition or inferred intent. Aliases shall be reviewable, editable and removable, persist across restarts and follow device/entity renames where a stable identity can be verified; if identity changes cannot be safely resolved, request clarification. Detect ambiguous or conflicting aliases across devices, areas and groups, and ask which target is intended rather than guessing. Alias resolution shall never bypass normal device permissions, Guest Mode restrictions, capability validation, or explicit device and room references. Tests shall cover multiple aliases, spoken alias creation and confirmation, ambiguity, collisions, entity renames, removal, guest access and safe fallback.
- **FR-045G [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **state-aware commands and questions** using current Home Assistant entity states, including “turn off any lights that are still on downstairs” and “tell me which windows are open”. Resolve requested area/floor/group and device class dynamically, query live states and act only on entities whose verified state matches the condition; do not use remembered or assumed states as substitutes for current data. For state queries, identify matching entities accurately and distinguish open, closed, unavailable and unknown states; do not claim a window sensor exists when none is configured. For state-filtered actions, exclude unavailable or ambiguous entities, respect device capabilities, protected entities, Guest Mode permissions and explicit exceptions, and revalidate targets before action. Where live data is stale, missing or inconsistent, explain the limitation and request clarification where necessary rather than guessing. Report partial success and failures accurately, with tests for mixed entity states, stale/unavailable sensors, area changes, exclusions, zero unintended actions and truthful responses.
- **FR-045H [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall recognise **natural-language intent and paraphrases for established household routines**, without requiring an exact routine name. For example, “we're going to watch a film” may resolve to the existing Movie Time routine, and “we're heading to bed” may resolve to Good Night. Resolve candidates using the current, authorised Home Assistant routine registry and approved learned phrase mappings, not speculative invented routines. Treat incidental punctuation and normal conversational variants as equivalent (FR-030); allow users to teach and correct routine paraphrases with confirmation (FR-038). If a phrase could reasonably mean several routines or a normal device action, **clarify before executing**; do not trigger routines from casual mentions or quotations. Check speaker permissions, Guest Mode restrictions, prerequisites and routine availability before execution; report actual success or partial failure accurately and never claim an unavailable routine ran. Respect explicit negations and in-utterance corrections (FR-034, FR-045B). Tests shall cover Movie Time/Good Night paraphrases, ambiguity, corrections, false-positive prevention, speaker/guest permissions and missing routines.
- **FR-045I [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **one-time overrides and exclusions when invoking an existing household routine**, such as “start Movie Time, but leave the lounge wall lights on at 10%” or “run Good Night, but don't turn off the bedroom fan”. Before executing the routine, resolve the requested overrides against the routine's actual actions, dependencies and current Home Assistant entities; apply an explicit exclusion **before any affected routine step executes**, rather than running it and undoing it afterward. An override applies **only to that invocation** and must not modify the saved routine, script, automation or future executions. For supported value overrides, validate the device capability and requested setting and arrange the affected action so the requested final state is honoured without unintended intermediate changes. If the routine is opaque, cannot be safely modified, or an override conflicts with a safety-critical or mandatory step, **do not claim the override can be guaranteed**: clarify or decline the modified execution rather than silently running the unmodified routine. Respect Guest Mode permissions, security constraints, in-utterance corrections, and exception handling (FR-034, FR-045B–C); report partial failures truthfully. Tests shall cover one-time exclusions, brightness overrides, no side effects on excluded devices, unchanged routine definitions, conflicts, opaque routines and failure handling.
- **FR-045J [Owner-approved policy; implementation pending]** Declan shall support **temporary device-state overrides with automatic restoration**, including “set the lounge lights to 20% for the next hour, then put them back” and “turn the heating up to 22°C for 30 minutes, then restore the previous temperature”. Before changing anything, capture the relevant **verified original state and attributes** (such as light on/off and brightness, or heating setpoint and operating mode), validate the target and requested duration, and clearly confirm the intended temporary change and planned restoration. Persist the pending restoration safely across Home Assistant restarts, independently of conversational context (FR-040), and avoid duplicate restoration. At expiry, restore only if the device still reflects Declan's temporary setting and no intervening manual, voice or automation change has superseded it; **never overwrite a newer intentional adjustment**.
